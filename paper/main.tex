\begin{abstract}
Reward models (RMs) are reported to score worse in lower-resource languages, but benchmarks that translate English
items cannot tell whether the drop comes from the \emph{language} or from \emph{India-specific content} the model
lacks in any language. We introduce a $2\times3$ factorial audit (India-specific vs generic facts $\times$ English,
Hindi, Telugu) on minimal preference pairs with identical content across languages, English facts checked against
cited sources and translations post-edited by a native speaker. [RESULT SENTENCE: fill after scoring: models,
$N$ per domain, language gap, context gap, interaction with intervals.] We release the dataset, code and a
pre-registered analysis.
\end{abstract}
